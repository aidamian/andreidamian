# Search and AI discovery: evidence and scope

Reviewed 18 September 2026 for the personal homepage and PurpleRay landing page. Sources below are original research papers and official provider guidance. This is a bounded implementation review, not a systematic literature review or a promise of improved rankings.

## Decision

Keep the existing design and readable HTML. Improve identity metadata, accurate structured data, source links, and crawl reliability. These changes make the site easier to identify, access, and verify. They do not establish that Google, Bing, or an AI answer service will rank or cite it.

Do not add `llms.txt` for SEO, synthetic reviews, hidden instructions to AI systems, repeated keywords, or mechanically expanded FAQ pages.

## What provider guidance establishes

- **Google Search:** ordinary indexing and snippet eligibility remain necessary for AI Overviews and AI Mode. Important information should be available as text, internally linked, and accessible through the CDN as well as `robots.txt`. Eligibility does not guarantee indexing or display. [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features).
- **Current inclusion control:** Google reports worldwide rollout on 31 August 2026 of Search Console's Settings > Search generative AI control. Include is the default; child properties can inherit a parent's choice. Confirm the effective property setting when account access is available. This controls AI-feature inclusion, not ordinary ranking or AI training. [Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024).
- **Google's newer guidance, introduced May 2026 and updated July:** Google Search ignores `llms.txt` and similar special AI files for visibility and ranking. Mandatory content chunking and AI-specific rewrites are unnecessary. Keep page length and organization appropriate for readers. [Generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).
- **Agent navigation is a different use case:** Chrome's Lighthouse agentic-browsing documentation describes `llms.txt` as an optional emerging convention. A missing file returning 404 receives N/A, not a failure. This does not contradict Google's search guidance or demonstrate a ranking benefit. A maintained directory might help some agents on a large documentation site; it adds little to these two linked HTML pages. [Lighthouse llms.txt audit](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/llms-txt).
- **Structured data:** Google uses it to understand content and determine eligibility for supported search presentations. JSON-LD is convenient, but the visible page must support its claims. There is no special AI schema requirement. [Structured data introduction](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data).
- **Profile markup:** `ProfilePage` with a `Person` main entity fits a page primarily about its own author. Google's guidance includes author and About Me pages. Stable identity links can clarify which Andrei Damian the page describes. [ProfilePage guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page).
- **Application markup:** `SoftwareApplication` can describe PurpleRay accurately. Google's software-app rich result additionally requires an offer price and a genuine rating or review. Free software may use a price of zero. Having truthful schema without those review fields is preferable to inventing data to satisfy rich-result checks; basic schema validity and Google rich-result eligibility are different checks. [SoftwareApplication guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/software-app).
- **Bing:** its guidelines emphasize explicit facts, consistent entity names, semantic headings, and structured data that matches visible content. They do not guarantee grounding traffic. Attempts to manipulate Bing/Copilot through prompt injection can reduce visibility or cause removal. [Bing webmaster guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a). The current article is indexed in search; its JavaScript-driven direct reader returned an empty shell during this review.

## What the empirical studies establish

| Study and status | Result relevant here | Limit on the inference |
|---|---|---|
| [Aggarwal et al., GEO, KDD 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3) | Adding relevant citations, quotations, or statistics changed source visibility; the paper reports gains up to 40% and a separate Perplexity experiment. | Main experiments rewrite a source already among five retrieved pages, then generate answers with GPT-3.5. Visibility combines answer attribution/position metrics. This does not demonstrate organic retrieval gains or a 40% increase in visits to a real personal website. Effects vary by source position and domain. |
| [Bagga et al., E-GEO, July 2026 revision](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20867v2), preprint | Tests product-description rewrites with 13,747 queries, ten retrieved Amazon listings per query, and five LLM re-rankers. Content changes can affect recommendation order in that setting. | The authors explicitly condition results on the fixed candidate-generation pipeline and Amazon data. Rankings produced by model experiments are not measured purchases or field-tested Google/ChatGPT discovery. A free SBOM application and researcher biography are different settings. |
| [Zhang et al., citation selection and absorption, April 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25707), preprint | Across 602 prompts and several AI search platforms, citation breadth differs from how much a cited source contributes to an answer. Pages with greater influence tend to have clear structure and extractable evidence. | This is descriptive observational analysis. Longer pages and structured evidence correlate with its influence measures; the analysis does not show that adding length, schema, or citations causes better ranking. |

These papers support testing whether useful, factual passages are represented accurately in answers. They do not justify a universal GEO score, a promised citation uplift, or copying optimization prompts into public pages. No reviewed primary experiment establishes a causal search-ranking benefit from `llms.txt` or JSON-LD on this kind of site.

## Small improvements for these pages

1. **State identity and purpose plainly.** Keep the full name, current Ratio1 role, and carefully separated Lummetry role/acquisition dates visible. Preserve consistent names across titles, headings, identity links, and schema. PurpleRay's opening description should identify it as an open-source desktop SBOM analyzer, with supported platforms and the macOS build pause stated accurately.
2. **Make significant claims checkable.** Keep nearby links to the acquisition announcement, original publications, and project repository. Prefer exact publication status, dates, product limitations, and platform requirements over extra promotional text. Citations help readers verify the claims; ranking improvement remains unproven.
3. **Keep essential information in the served HTML.** Biography, product description, screenshots, documentation links, and release fallbacks should remain usable without JavaScript. Dynamic package choices and statistics should retain their honest unavailable states. A package download total is not a user count or rating.
4. **Use a small, truthful entity graph.** Connect the profile to the person and verified public identities; connect PurpleRay to its author, source repository, license, and visible features. Do not add credentials, organization relationships, application versions, or platform support that the visible content cannot support.
5. **Verify access and canonical URLs.** Check real GET responses for both pages, assets, `robots.txt`, and the sitemap. Avoid Cloudflare challenges or redirects that prevent retrieval. Keep one canonical URL per page and ordinary crawlable links between them. No separate subdomain is needed for AI discoverability.

## Measure without overstating causality

Use Search Console for indexing, impressions, queries, and clicks. Its newer **Generative AI performance report** reports impressions from AI Overviews and AI Mode, with page, country, device, and date dimensions. Google's help page says worldwide rollout completed on 31 August 2026; insufficient impressions can still prevent a report from appearing. This supersedes the older December 2025 AI-features page's description of combined Web reporting alone. The new report is not a universal GEO rank or a report of all chatbot usage. [Google's current report documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139).

Use Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report when available to inspect cited pages and grounding queries. Bing explicitly says these are citations, not visits, ranking, or proof that an individual edit caused a change. [Bing AI Performance](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/ai-performance-9f8e7d6c).

For occasional manual checks, record the date, engine, query wording, whether live search occurred, cited URLs, and factual errors. Repeat a small fixed set of identity and PurpleRay questions with paraphrases; a single successful answer is weak evidence. Compare human traffic and useful actions separately from citation counts. No additional tracking script or paid GEO service is necessary for the initial implementation.
